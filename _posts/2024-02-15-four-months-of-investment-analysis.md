I just wrapped up a few months doing investment analysis at a fund in Beijing, mostly on education- and trust-fund-related projects. Most of the actual work was less "finance" than I expected and more "cleaning and reading a lot of numbers until a pattern shows up" — pulling apart 10,000+ rows of data in SQL and Excel, cross-checking them against policy changes and industry trends, and trying to turn all of that into something a portfolio decision could actually rest on.

The part I spent the most time on was a report on the study-abroad industry — figuring out where the actual investable opportunities were versus where the narrative just sounded good. It was a useful lesson in how much of "analysis" is really just deciding which numbers to trust and which ones are noise dressed up as a trend.

I don't know yet whether I want to end up back in finance specifically, or somewhere more technical, but this was a good first real exposure to using data to argue for a decision instead of just describing it.
