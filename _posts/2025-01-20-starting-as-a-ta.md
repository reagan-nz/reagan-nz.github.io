Started TA-ing for NYU's Data Structures course this semester. I'm running weekly office hours and grading assignments that cover linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, and hash tables — the usual first real pass at data structures most CS students go through.

The part I didn't expect: explaining recursion well is harder than writing recursive code well. Most students can eventually get a recursive function to work by trial and error, but watching someone try to trace through a call stack by hand is a good reminder of how much intuition you build up without noticing, and how little of it transfers automatically when you're the one trying to explain it.

I'm hoping this turns into a habit of explaining things more carefully in general, not just in office hours.
